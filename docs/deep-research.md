# Turn one phone demo into hundreds

The best plan for the next ~52 hours is this. Use the iPhone's LiDAR depth and ARKit poses to turn a few of your own recordings of "bowl onto plate" into metric, object-relative gripper paths. Replay them in the LIBERO task `KITCHEN_SCENE1_put_the_black_bowl_on_the_plate`. Multiply each successful replay into 100 to 200 sim demos by moving the objects (MimicGen style). Then train a cheap ACT policy on the laptop and a SmolVLA fine-tune on a Modal L4, and measure both with 100+ seeded rollouts against honest baselines. This beats the obvious version, which has already been tried and failed in public. A previous applicant used a monocular chest-mounted phone and needed hand-tuned 8x gains to get a 3.84 cm lift in a single rollout ([GitHub](https://github.com/AyushS15/Humanoid-challenge)). The two failure points that sink this kind of project are scale and frame errors and too few demos. Metric depth fixes the first and object-centric augmentation fixes the second. The field agrees that getting the actions right matters more than making the video look like a robot ([EgoEngine](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.12604v1), [HuRo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10706)). A policy trained on sim renders of retargeted replays never sees a hand, so the "hand vs robot" visual gap simply disappears. For the "creative step", the strongest fit for a PPO user is a small residual RL controller on top of a state-based policy. It only makes sense if the base policy succeeds sometimes but not always. A world model is the weakest use of the remaining hours.

## The recipe is standard; what stands out is metric depth, augmentation and honest numbers

Every 2025 to 2026 human-video-to-robot paper uses the same chain. It estimates a 3D hand, places a virtual parallel gripper at the midpoint of the thumb and index fingertips, sets the gripper's open or closed state from the pinch distance, then trains an imitation policy ([HumanEgo](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.24934v3), [Phantom](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.00779)). None of the named works (Phantom, HumanEgo, HuRo, Ego2Robot, EgoEngine, R+X, EgoMimic) evaluates on LIBERO, and most use Aria glasses rather than phones ([prior-art check of each arXiv page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.00779)). The closest LIBERO work is UniSkill, where people filmed LIBERO-like tasks and those videos served only as *prompts* to a policy trained on 50 robot demos per task. With human prompts it reached **0.48** success versus 0.91 with robot prompts ([UniSkill](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.08787)). So "my phone video becomes the LIBERO training data" is open in the literature, but reviewers have already seen applicants attempt it.

Three numbers set the bar. First, naive replay of human trajectories in sim mostly fails: plain IK replay scores **0.10 to 0.17**, while feasibility-aware correction scores 0.83 to 0.90 ([EgoEngine](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.12604v1)). Second, monocular hand tracking "drops real-world success sharply" ([HumanEgo](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.24934v3)), and without depth, hand overlays carry **3 to 4 cm** of error along the viewing direction ([Phantom repo](https://github.com/MarionLepert/phantom)). LIBERO's bowl-on-plate check allows only 3 cm, so that error alone can decide success. Third, object-centric segment replay turned about 200 human demos into more than 50,000 ([MimicGen](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.17596)). The previous applicant's repo shows the cost of ignoring all three. It used monocular video, CoTracker3 tracks, deltas scaled 8x, and a custom robosuite environment (not LIBERO, despite a file named `libero_runner.py`). It reported single rollouts only, and the plain SmolVLA fine-tune lifted the glass **0.00 cm** ([GitHub](https://github.com/AyushS15/Humanoid-challenge)). A second repo reportedly converts human video to LIBERO-format demos but was not opened ([GitHub](https://github.com/tristanengelborghs/robot), unverified).

What stands out, then, is fixing the known failure points and measuring them. Use metric 3D from LiDAR with no gain tuning. Express the motion relative to the objects, not the camera. Multiply demos. Run a real LIBERO task with its built-in success check. Report success rates over many seeded rollouts with confidence intervals. A cheap ablation that almost nobody shows is replay success with LiDAR depth versus monocular depth on the same recordings. It directly proves why your data pipeline matters. Cross-embodiment tricks and Phantom-style inpainting add little here: the sim render already shows only the robot, and in EgoEngine's comparison Phantom scored 0.00 to 0.05 on real tasks ([EgoEngine](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.12604v1)).

## Hand to gripper in nine steps, with LiDAR doing the hard part

Stray Scanner records several files per take. `rgb.mp4` holds the color video. `depth/` holds one 16-bit PNG per RGB frame at **256x192 in millimetres**, and `confidence/` holds per-pixel confidence of 0, 1 or 2. `odometry.csv` gives per-frame position, quaternion and intrinsics `fx, fy, cx, cy` ([Stray format](https://github.com/strayrobots/scanner/blob/main/docs/format.md)). The official doc does not state the pose convention. An unofficial page says it is OpenCV-like camera axes ([Notion](https://excellent-legal-071.notion.site/Stray-Scanner-Data-Model-aea95b702b484d41b2de12d2e90781ce)), and the earlier notes say `~/spatial-project` already solved this loader. The intrinsics are probably for the 1920x1440 RGB image, so multiply by 0.1333 for depth pixels. That scale is unverified; check that `cx` is about 960.

The recipe:

1. **Hands.** Run WiLoR-mini (pip install; models CC-BY-NC-ND) or HaMeR (MIT; used by Phantom) per frame at 15 to 30 fps to get 21 3D keypoints and a MANO mesh ([WiLoR](https://github.com/rolpotamias/WiLoR), [HaMeR](https://github.com/geopavlakos/hamer)). Both need the free `MANO_RIGHT.pkl` registration. Neither publishes VRAM figures, so "fits 8 GB" is an estimate. MediaPipe is the zero-install fallback. Lift its 2D thumb and index tips directly with depth.
2. **Depth alignment.** Shift the predicted hand mesh so its median depth matches the median LiDAR depth of the hand pixels with confidence 2. Optionally refine with Open3D ICP at a 1 to 2 cm threshold, as Phantom does with SAM2 masks ([Phantom](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.00779)).
3. **World frame.** Transform each frame's points with that frame's ARKit pose.
4. **Gripper position.** Use the midpoint of the thumb and index tips.
5. **Gripper orientation.** For a bowl, skip the hand's orientation entirely. Command robosuite's top-down gripper `[[0,1,0],[1,0,0],[0,0,-1]]` and keep at most the yaw ([robosuite osc.py](https://github.com/ARISE-Initiative/robosuite/blob/v1.4.0/robosuite/controllers/osc.py)). If you need full orientation, HumanEgo's frame is the stable choice: x from thumb-MCP to index-MCP, y from wrist toward the MCP midpoint ([HumanEgo](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.24934)).
6. **Grasp.** Compute thumb-index distance per frame. Treat the bottom 20th percentile of each take as "closed" (Phantom's rule) and send +1 for closed, -1 for open.
7. **Smoothing (HumanEgo's values at 30 Hz).** Drop keypoints with confidence below 0.8. Fill gaps of up to 10 frames by interpolation. Apply Savitzky-Golay with window 21 and order 2. Median-filter the grasp signal.
8. **Table frame.** RANSAC-fit the table plane with Open3D `segment_plane(0.01, 3, 1000)`. Get the bowl and plate positions at the first and last frame from a click-prompted SAM 2 mask plus the median of the masked depth points.
9. **Object-relative output.** Save the approach-and-grasp segment relative to the bowl and the carry-and-place segment relative to the plate.

Two limits matter. This mapping works for pinch grasps only, so **record yourself pinching the bowl rim** with one hand, slowly, with the hand visible. Also, your real bowl is not the size of LIBERO's `akita_black_bowl`. Plan to snap the grasp height and rim offset to the sim bowl, and let the human supply the approach path, timing and place motion. Mesh sizes were not checked, so this is a known gap.

## LIBERO: pick KITCHEN_SCENE1 bowl-on-plate and drive it with closed-loop deltas

S1 already passed on the laptop. lerobot 0.6.1, robosuite 1.4.0 and mujoco 3.8.1 installed, and one environment ran at **42 steps/s at 256x256** (107 steps/s at 128x128). Each step reports ground-truth bowl and plate poses. The one open item is cheap: pin `mujoco<3.4`. From 3.4 onward the reset settling changes, and a stored libero_spatial bowl ends up tilted ([lerobot #4390](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/issues/4390)). That mainly matters for zero-shot baselines on stored benchmark init states.

**Task.** `KITCHEN_SCENE1_put_the_black_bowl_on_the_plate` (libero_90) declares only a bowl, a plate and a fixed cabinet. Its goal is `(On akita_black_bowl_1 plate_1)` ([BDDL](https://github.com/Lifelong-Robot-Learning/LIBERO/blob/master/libero/libero/bddl_files/libero_90/KITCHEN_SCENE1_put_the_black_bowl_on_the_plate.bddl)). There is a second, less obvious advantage. LeRobot's LIBERO training set covers only the 40 spatial, object, goal and 10 tasks ([LeRobot docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/lerobot/libero)), so `smolvla_libero` has not trained on this exact task. A zero-shot baseline is therefore informative rather than near ceiling (inferred from task counts, unverified). The alternative is libero_goal `put_the_bowl_on_the_plate`, which has ready LeRobot demos but more clutter, and a pretrained model may already solve it, which would hide your data's contribution. One subtlety: the BDDL spawn ranges are tiny, with the bowl and plate each within **±2.5 cm**. Under the default spawns even blind replay may succeed. Evaluate on both the default spawn and a widened one (for example ±10 cm with yaw) that you sample yourself.

**Placing objects.** After `reset()`, write each object's free joint with `sim.data.set_joint_qpos(obj.joints[-1], [x,y,z,qw,qx,qy,qz])`, call `sim.forward()`, then step 10 no-op actions `[0,0,0,0,0,0,-1]` to let things settle. MuJoCo uses wxyz quaternion order and robosuite utilities use xyzw, a classic bug. Keep the z the reset gave you ([bddl_base_domain.py](https://github.com/Lifelong-Robot-Learning/LIBERO/blob/master/libero/libero/envs/bddl_base_domain.py), [lerobot libero.py](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/blob/main/src/lerobot/envs/libero.py)).

**Driving the arm.** The controller is OSC_POSE at 20 Hz. In the default delta mode, an action of 1.0 means **5 cm** of translation or **0.5 rad** of rotation from the *current* pose, and the gripper is -1 open, +1 closed ([osc_pose.json](https://github.com/ARISE-Initiative/robosuite/blob/v1.4.0/robosuite/controllers/config/osc_pose.json)). Follow your trajectory in closed loop, as MimicGen does. At each step compute `clip((target - current)/0.05, -1, 1)` for position and the axis-angle of `R_target @ R_cur.T` divided by 0.5 for rotation ([mimicgen robosuite.py](https://github.com/NVlabs/mimicgen/blob/main/mimicgen/env_interfaces/robosuite.py)). Resample to 20 Hz, keep steps to 1 to 2 cm, and hold 5 to 10 steps after closing the gripper before lifting.

**Success check.** `env.check_success()` evaluates the goal every step. "On" means the bowl is above the plate, the two touch, and their centers are **under 3 cm apart in xy** ([predicates](https://github.com/Lifelong-Robot-Learning/LIBERO/blob/master/libero/libero/envs/object_states/base_object_states.py)). Read the plate center from the sim at run time, and keep stepping after release until the check holds.

**Demo format.** Write a LeRobotDataset v3.0 that matches `lerobot/libero` exactly: `observation.images.image` (agentview) and `observation.images.image2` (wrist) at 256x256, an 8-D `observation.state` (EE position, axis-angle, 2 gripper joints), a 7-D `action`, and the task string ([info.json](https://huggingface.co/datasets/lerobot/libero/blob/main/meta/info.json)). Apply LeRobot's 180-degree image flip, and visually compare one of your frames with a `lerobot/libero` frame ([env_processor.py](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/blob/main/src/lerobot/processor/env_processor.py)). The dataset metadata says **10 fps** while the env runs at **20 Hz** ([configs.py](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/blob/main/src/lerobot/envs/configs.py)). This discrepancy is unexplained, so pick 20 and keep it consistent. Also log `get_sim_state()` per step so you can re-render later.

**Augmentation.** MimicGen does not support LIBERO out of the box ([mimicgen install](https://github.com/NVlabs/mimicgen/blob/main/docs/introduction/installation.md)), but its core is one formula you can write in about 200 lines. Sample new bowl and plate poses. Transform the grasp segment by `T_bowl_new · T_bowl_src⁻¹ · T_eef` and the place segment by the plate's equivalent. Insert 5 interpolation steps between segments, copy the gripper commands, and **keep only rollouts where `check_success()` fires** ([MimicGen](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.17596), [pose_utils.py](https://github.com/NVlabs/mimicgen/blob/main/mimicgen/utils/pose_utils.py)). The bowl is round, so its yaw barely matters, and yield should be high (an estimate). At the measured 42 steps/s, a 200 to 300 step episode takes about 5 to 7 s per process. With 16 to 24 processes on 32 cores, 200 successes should take minutes, assuming throughput scales with cores (unverified; measure it).

## Policy choice: ACT locally for speed, SmolVLA on Modal for the VLA story

| Option | Fits 8 GB? | LIBERO evidence | Role here |
|---|---|---|---|
| State MLP with object poses | yes, minutes | n/a (uses sim-only information) | pipeline check, RL base |
| ACT | yes, ~2 to 6 GB at batch 8 | no primary LIBERO number | fast vision baseline |
| Diffusion Policy | borderline, ~8 to 14 GB | 72.4% avg multi-task | skip |
| SmolVLA (450M) | only at batch 1 to 4, untested | 87.3% paper, **76.35%** public checkpoint | main VLA result, on Modal |
| pi0.5 / GR00T / OpenVLA-OFT | no, 22.5 to 70+ GB to fine-tune | 96 to 97.5% | cite only |

The VRAM figures come from LeRobot's hardware guide ([guide](https://huggingface.co/docs/lerobot/en/hardware_guide.md)). The SmolVLA paper's 87.3% used a 100k-step, batch-64 fine-tune. An independent 2,000-rollout check of the public checkpoint measured **76.35%**, which the authors attribute to a release trained on one eighth of the sample budget ([SmolVLA](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.01844v1), [arXiv 2609.15940](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.15940)). Fine-tune from `lerobot/smolvla_libero`, not `smolvla_base`. It already knows LIBERO's cameras, state and actions, while the base model was pretrained on SO-100 arm data.

Three practical traps apply. First, you have lerobot 0.6.1, but the `--accelerator.mixed_precision` and gradient-accumulation flags exist only on `main` ([accelerator.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/huggingface/lerobot/main/src/lerobot/configs/accelerator.py)). Local SmolVLA training is therefore a gamble, and Modal is the sane path. An L4 costs about **$0.80/h** and an A100-40GB about $2.10/h, from per-second rates, and the Starter plan includes $30/month of credit ([Modal pricing](https://modal.com/pricing)). A 10k to 20k step run at 3 to 6 h on an L4 should cost $3 to 5 (an estimate). Second, `smolvla_libero` ships `n_action_steps=50`, which reportedly costs **about 20 points**, so evaluate with 10 ([#4614](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/issues/4614)). Third, fine-tuning can keep the base checkpoint's stale `input_features` ([#4517](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/issues/4517)).

**Demos needed.** LeRobot recommends about 50 episodes for SmolVLA and says 25 "was not enough" ([SmolVLA docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/lerobot/smolvla)). Since generated demos are nearly free, train on **200**, spread over the widened spawn range. Your phone data then plays a clear and measurable role: it is the seed for every demo.

**Evaluation protocol.** Write your own eval loop with fixed seeds, and run every condition on the same seeded object poses under both the default and the widened spawn distribution. Use **100 episodes per condition and 200 for headline numbers**. One of the research notes claims 50 episodes give ±7 points. The binomial math says otherwise: at 50% success with n=50, the 95% interval is about **±14 points**, falling to about ±10 at n=100 and ±7 at n=200. Sim episodes are cheap, so pay for the bigger n.

The conditions to compare are:

- open-loop replay of each raw phone demo
- the same replay with monocular depth instead of LiDAR (the ablation)
- zero-shot `smolvla_libero`
- ACT on 200 generated demos
- SmolVLA fine-tuned on 200 generated demos
- optionally, a curve of success against the number of generated demos (10, 50, 200) and the number of phone seeds (1 vs 5)

## RL fits only as a small residual, and a world model is a stretch

Published RL results on LIBERO VLAs are striking but out of reach. SimpleVLA-RL took 1-demo OpenVLA-OFT from **17.3% to 91.7%** on 8x A800 80 GB ([GitHub](https://github.com/PRIME-RL/SimpleVLA-RL)). RLinf used 8 to 32 H100s ([arXiv 2510.06710](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.06710)). DSRL lifted pi0 on LIBERO from about 20% to about 100% in about 10,000 online samples by training only a small policy over the base model's input noise ([DSRL](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15799v2)), but pi0 does not fit 8 GB. SmolVLA is also a flow-matching model, so the DSRL idea transfers in principle. No source has tested it, and SmolVLA's laptop inference speed is unmeasured, so treat it as a gamble.

The version that fits works like this. Train a small state-based policy on proprioception plus the sim's object poses, and label it clearly as using sim-only information. Freeze it. Add a residual network whose output is bounded to 10 to 20% of the action range and starts near zero, and train it on LIBERO's sparse success reward, optionally with reach and grasp shaping ([Policy Decorator](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.13630)). Sample budgets decide the algorithm. Off-policy residual learning (ResFiT style SAC, 50/50 demo and online replay) converged in about **75k to 200k steps** on robomimic Franka tasks. The PPO residual comparison needed about **40M** ([ResFiT](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19301v2)), and the original PPO-residual work ran 1,024 parallel GPU environments ([ResiP](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.16677v4)). LIBERO throughput without rendering on 32 cores is not published. An estimate of 3k to 10k steps/s aggregate puts PPO at 10M+ steps within reach overnight and SAC within an hour (unverified; measure first). RL only shows anything if the base succeeds **10 to 70%** of the time. If the augmented ACT already scores 95% on the default spawns, point RL at the widened spawns. A cleaner framing is to compare two ways of amplifying a single phone demo: augmentation versus residual RL.

A world model is the weakest bet. Phone video has no robot actions, so making it "action-conditioned" requires pseudo-actions or latent actions, a full research step on its own ([LAPA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.11758)). Cosmos-Predict2 2B needs about **32.5 GB** just for inference ([model matrix](https://docs.nvidia.com/cosmos/latest/predict2/model_matrix.html)). DINO-WM's ~19M-parameter predictor trains on a 4070, but on PushT its planning succeeded only **8%** of the time at 200 trajectories and 48% at 1,000 ([DINO-WM](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.04983)). If you want a world-model touch, train a small DINO-feature dynamics model on your own sim rollouts. Report prediction error against horizon, and check whether it ranks 3 policy checkpoints in the same order as true LIBERO success ([WorldGym](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.00613)). Do it only after a main result exists.

### First test to run tonight (all plans)

Record one 20-second Stray take of the bowl and plate on the table, then measure the bowl-center-to-plate-center distance with a tape. Unproject the table depth from frame 0 and the last frame into world coordinates using the per-frame poses. Then lift the SAM 2 or clicked bowl and plate centers.

**Pass** if two conditions hold: the two table point clouds overlap within **1 cm** (median plane distance), and the LiDAR-measured bowl-to-plate distance matches the tape within **2 cm**. **Fail** on either one: fix the pose convention (try the inverse, or a `diag(1,-1,-1)` flip) and the intrinsics scale before writing any hand code. Every later step assumes this metric frame is right.

Plan 2 also needs a 10-minute throughput test. Run 24 LIBERO processes with cameras off and random actions. At **≥ 2,000 steps/s** aggregate, PPO residual is viable overnight; below that, use SAC only.

## Conclusion

The challenge rewards showing that your own data matters. The design that does this best treats the phone as a source of *metric, object-relative motion*, not of pixels, and lets the simulator supply the robot images. That reframing turns the previous applicant's open problems (scale guessing, gain tuning, one rollout) into measured quantities. It also gives a clean causal story for the README: LiDAR depth versus no depth, 1 seed versus many, augmentation versus RL. Policy choice matters less than demo quality and count. A 200-demo ACT with honest 200-episode intervals will impress more than a SmolVLA fine-tune reported from a handful of rollouts.

Three uncertainties remain. Real versus sim bowl geometry has not been checked. Parallel throughput and SmolVLA laptop inference speed are unmeasured. It is also unknown how well `smolvla_libero` transfers zero-shot to the libero_90 bowl task. Each one is settled by a test of an hour or less, so run those before committing to the gate.
